# GRISSA

### Green Research Infrastructure Sustainability Self Assessment

**GRISSA** helps research infrastructures, datacenters, digital infrastructures, and organisations understand where they stand on sustainability — and what to improve next.

It turns a structured self-assessment into a shared **capability baseline**: clear maturity levels, priority gaps, a practical action plan, and a stakeholder-ready report. GRISSA is developed in the context of **GreenDIGIT** and related open digital infrastructure collaboration.

> **GRISSA is not a certification system.**  
> It does not award certificates or decide compliance. It supports preparation and planning so teams can get ready for audits, standards pathways, regulatory expectations, or internal sustainability goals.

## Why it matters

Sustainability for research and digital infrastructures is rarely a single checklist. Practices span governance, energy, monitoring, circularity, water, and lifecycle decisions — often owned by different teams, documented in different places, and hard to compare over time.

Without a shared structure, organisations struggle to:

- Agree on a **common picture** of current capability
- Separate “urgent gaps” from longer-term improvements
- Brief leadership or partners with something more useful than a slide deck of anecdotes
- Prepare for **standards alignment**, certification pathways, or regulatory readiness with a documented baseline

GRISSA addresses that need. It offers a guided, theme-based self-assessment so teams can record where they are today, see where they want to be, and leave with a prioritised plan — not just a score.

---

## Who it is for

GRISSA is designed for organisations that operate or support **research and digital infrastructures**, including:

- **Research Infrastructures (RIs)** and related facilities  
- **Datacenters** and compute / storage environments  
- **Digital infrastructures** and platform providers  
- **Organisations** coordinating sustainability, compliance, or improvement programmes across those environments  

Typical participants include sustainability leads, facility or operations managers, energy and environmental specialists, governance / compliance roles, and leadership who need a clear baseline for planning discussions.

## What you get

| Outcome | What it means in practice |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Results overview** | Completion status, capability levels across themes, and whether optional targets are met |
| **Priority summary** | Improvement needs classified as **High**, **Medium**, or **Low** so attention goes to the right places first |
| **Action plan** | Recommended actions with implementation priority and effort estimates — a starting agenda, not a vague wish list |
| **Interactive dashboard** | A visual view of theme performance and priorities for discussion and tracking |
| **Print-friendly PDF report** | A structured export for stakeholders, reviews, and certification-**baseline** conversations |

Together, these outputs form a **readiness baseline**: a shared reference point for internal strategy, standards discussion, and next-step planning.

## What GRISSA covers

The assessment is organised around **seven themes**. Teams choose which areas are in scope for a given assessment, so the exercise stays relevant to their context.

| Theme | Focus |
|-------|--------|
| **Governance and Compliance** | Policies, responsibilities, and how sustainability is steered and evidenced |
| **Stakeholder Engagement and Awareness** | Communication, participation, and building shared understanding |
| **Energy Efficiency and Decarbonisation** | Energy use, efficiency measures, and pathways toward lower carbon impact |
| **Environmental Monitoring and Impact** | Measuring, tracking, and understanding environmental performance |
| **Pollution, Waste, and Circularity** | Waste, materials, and circular approaches |
| **Water and Ecosystems** | Water use and related ecosystem considerations |
| **Lifecycle Integration** | Sustainability across procurement, operations, and lifecycle decisions |

Within each in-scope area, questions use a clear **capability scale (typically Level 1–3)**. You can also set **optional target levels** to express ambition — for example, where you want to be for a planning horizon or programme goal.

## How an assessment works

GRISSA follows a deliberate path from scope to report. You can save progress and return later; nothing requires completing everything in one sitting.

```text
  Scope & goals  →  Questionnaire  →  Results  →  Action plan  →  Dashboard & report
```

### Step 1 — Define scope and goals

Choose which topic areas apply to this assessment. Set optional **target capability levels** where you want a clear ambition against today’s practice.

### Step 2 — Answer the assessment

For each in-scope question, rate current capability (typically **1–3**). The questionnaire is structured so answers stay comparable across themes and over time.

### Step 3 — Review results

See completion, capability levels, targets met, and where gaps appear. Priorities help distinguish what needs attention now versus what can be planned next.

### Step 4 — Action plan, dashboard, and report

Generate a prioritised **action plan**, explore the **dashboard**, and export a **PDF report** for stakeholders. The report is designed for discussion and planning — as a baseline, not as a certificate.

In the application, the main navigation is simple:

- **Home** — introduction and orientation  
- **Workspace** — your assessments, team access, and versions

## A typical journey

Imagine a research infrastructure preparing for an internal sustainability review with partners and leadership.

1. A sustainability lead creates an assessment in the **Workspace** and invites an operations colleague as an **editor** and a facility manager as a **viewer**.
2. Together they set **scope** — energy, monitoring, and governance first; other themes later.
3. They answer the questionnaire over two workshops, saving progress between sessions.
4. The **dashboard** shows where capability is already solid and where high-priority gaps sit.
5. The team reviews the **action plan**, adjusts implementation notes where needed, and exports a **PDF report** for the review meeting.
6. Later, they create a new **version** of the assessment to capture progress without losing the earlier baseline.

That pattern — baseline → discuss → act → reassess — is what GRISSA is built to support.

## How to read the outputs

| Concept | How to use it |
|--------|----------------|
| **Capability levels (1–3)** | A structured view of maturity for each question or theme — not a pass/fail grade |
| **Optional targets** | Your chosen ambition; gaps show where current practice sits below that target |
| **High / Medium / Low priorities** | A practical ordering for improvement discussions |
| **Action plan & effort** | Recommended next steps with a sense of implementation weight — a planning aid, not a fixed project charter |
| **Interpretation text** | Stakeholder-oriented language to help explain the baseline in meetings and reports |

Use the outputs to agree on priorities, assign owners outside the tool, and track progress in follow-up assessments.

## Working with your team

Sustainability assessments rarely belong to one person. GRISSA supports shared work:

| Role | Intended use |
|------|----------------|
| **Owner** | Creates and manages the assessment; can invite others, manage versions, and delete when appropriate |
| **Editor** | Contributes to draft content and can help create versions |
| **Reviewer / Viewer** | Follows progress and results without editing content |

**Versions** let you keep a living draft while preserving snapshots over time (for example draft → submitted). Content changes are intended on **draft** versions so submitted baselines stay stable for reporting.

## What GRISSA is — and is not

| GRISSA **is** | GRISSA **is not** |
|---------------|-------------------|
| A structured **self-assessment** for sustainability maturity | A certification body or auditor |
| A way to build a shared **baseline** and action agenda | A guarantee of compliance with any standard |
| Support for standards-alignment and regulatory **readiness** discussions | A substitute for formal certification processes |
| A tool for internal planning, transparency, and stakeholder reporting | A one-size-fits-all mandate for every organisation |

Honesty about this boundary is intentional: it keeps the tool useful for planning while avoiding claims the self-assessment cannot make.
